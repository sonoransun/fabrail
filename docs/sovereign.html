Sovereign tier

When cost is no object, physics still sets the limits.

An unlimited budget buys margin, redundancy, better materials and more storage. It does not move the magnetic-pressure ceiling, the stroke bound on a damper, the warning time a sensor can buy, or the distance a blast needs to arrive late.

🧪 R Research tier. Architectures are designed toward safety-integrity targets; no certification at any SIL or PL is claimed. Every alert is synthetic or injected. Each class carries its badge and, separately, its technology maturity.

Not claimed on this page.

  • No certification at any SIL or PL. Ferrocene qualification of a compiler target does not certify an application, and one lockstep chip is SIL 3 at most.
  • Mass dampers are mainly wind devices: at design-level shaking they add at most about 0.7 % damping, and none acts before the first half-cycle of a near-fault pulse.
  • No maglev vertical isolation, no whole-building levitation; isolation platforms have no ASCE 7 Ch. 17 prototype tests and no code path, and the moat edge is a shear hazard.
  • No yield-to-distance blast tables and no scaled-distance law; fabrail is not a protective-design tool. The blast arrival time is always an input.
  • Tsunami timing only, no inundation modelling; buoyant and hydraulic gates beat maglev physically.
  • fabrail is not an early-warning provider; every feed is synthetic, and near the source the warning is about zero.
  • Simulation is not validation: no hardware, no FEM, and the storage, PFH and hover-loss rates are placeholders or closed forms.

Nine classes, safe by separation

At 500 t the pedestrian caps allow only 2.6 mm/s (1.69 J) or 0.045 mm/s (22.3 kg·m/s), so a sovereign mover cannot be safe by being slow. It is safe by keeping people out of reach while it moves (a guarded zone with PL e presence sensing, interlocks and ISO 13855 separation) or by carrying them within comfort limits (an occupied platform). The badge is fabrail's status; maturity is the technology's readiness in the world and never raises the badge.

SENTINEL: a 1,000 t damper in a supertall

maturity TRL 3–4 · tuning closed forms ✅ V

A hybrid mass damper of about 1 % of the tower's modal mass rides a curved guideway (the passive restoring force) and an ironless LSM trims and damps it. In wind it is tuned with Warburton's force-form optimum; in an earthquake it drops to SEISMIC_PASSIVE and rides through levitated, because landing would remove the damping it adds.

Host displacement RMS under white-noise force for the passive force-form optimum, computed from the Lyapunov equation of the 2-DOF model: 44.7 % at μ 1 %, ζs 1 %, but 30.0 % at ζs 2 % (EXOTIC §X6). The reduction is always printed with ζs; the ≥ 40 % target applies to the active mode only.

Why ironless: an iron-core LSM's cogging at 1–3 % of 121 kN is 0.12–0.37 mg on 1,000 t, which alone breaks the ≤ 0.3 mg activation target; and the rail must be low-coercivity or homopolar, because laminations cut eddy drag but not hysteresis drag (EXOTIC §X7, §X8).

HAVEN-R: an isolation plinth at T = 6 s

maturity TRL 2–3 · transmissibility closed forms ✅ V

A 50 t platform on a curved guideway of radius R = g·T²/4π² (8.95 m at 6 s): the friction-pendulum law without the friction. Skyhook damping acts on the platform's absolute velocity; recentring is weak and switched off while shaking; pre-positioning runs only with independent confirmation and a moat that is ClearVerified. Vertical isolation stays passive: a 10–20 mm gap cannot give the 0.25 m static deflection that 1 Hz needs.

SD = SD1·g·T/(4π²·B) and apeak = (SD1·g/(B·T))·√(1 + 4ζ²), with B interpolated between 1.35 (15 %), 1.5 (20 %) and 1.7 (30 %) damping: at T = 4 s the DBE peak is 0.108 g, over the 0.1 g occupant target; at 6 s it is 0.072 g. The 0.1 g figure is an analysis output for a stated site, not a trip (EXOTIC §X9; FAB-EXO-505).

ARENA: 8,600 t on 500 pods, one fails

maturity TRL 3 · static allocation only; dynamic simulation 🗺️ M

A stadium field tray carried by zero-power pods. When a pod fails, the rigid-body wrench is re-allocated by weighted least squares with active-set clamping, the algorithm of fabrail-exo's static allocator re-evaluated here in the browser. The tray lands when any quadrant's healthy capacity falls below 1.3× its weight share. The tray starts with one pod failed; click or tap pods to fail more.

BASTION: the closure race

maturity TRL 2–3 · closed forms ✅ V

A 50 t sliding plug closes 3 m on a jerk-limited profile and lands to seal. The planner answers one question, "can it finish in time": CLOSE iff tmin + tseal + margin ≤ ta − tdetect, with the zone clear or a two-person override active. The arrival time ta is always an input; the only distance fabrail reports is the bound a shock implies, R ≥ c0·ta, because a shock never travels slower than sound.

Inputs
–

3 m, j = 2 m/s³: 5.246 s at a = 0.48 m/s² (peak 1.144 m/s), 5.031 s at 0.53 m/s²; seal and margin 0.6 s; c0 = 343 m/s. A close therefore needs ta ≥ 5.85 s, a source at least about 2 km away. Nearby blasts arrive in 0.1–0.5 s, where only normally closed doors and blast valves act: the value of a fast close is less exposure while the door must be open (EXOTIC §X11).

TIDEGATE: time is not the problem

maturity TRL 2 (triggering TRL 7–9) · timing model 🔶 S

A 150 t gate on hybrid EMS with wheels as the fallback. It closes only after the shaking ends and only on the command of an external decision system, after a rail-gap check; if the check fails it closes on its wheels or rack. A tsunami crossing a shelf at √(g·h) leaves minutes; coastal subsidence, tilt, liquefaction and debris in the gap are the real objections.

Timeline after the origin, minutes
–

Arrival ∫dx/√(g·h) over a deep segment at 4,000 m and a shelf segment; 100 km over a 1,000 m shelf takes about 17 minutes. Green's law gives the amplitude growth (h₀/h₁)^¼ to a 10 m approach. The deep segment's depth, the rail-check time (30 s) and the wheel-close time (2× the maglev close) are illustrative inputs. The decision path has a SIL 3 target; SIL 4 is unclaimable (EXOTIC §X12).

Ride-through energy

closed form; the stores are simplified 🔶 S

A class that must stay levitated through an earthquake has to carry its hold power on islanded storage for Tride = max(300 s, the scenario's shaking) + landing. For hybrid EMS the permanent magnets carry the weight and the coils only the dynamic force, so the hold power scales with the square of the vertical acceleration at the suspension; pure EMS pays for the whole weight.

PW = 1.7 kW/t (the Transrapid levitation-and-guidance figure) and Phold = PW·(arms/2g)² for hybrid EMS: 10 kt at 0.2 g for 300 s is 170 kW and 51 MJ, 102 MJ with the margin, within one EMALS-class disk's demonstrated 121 MJ per cycle; pure EMS needs 5.1 GJ against a JET-class flywheel's ≈ 2.8 GJ usable. The admission also checks absorption (a full store cannot take braking energy). EXOTIC §X4, §X5.

1oo2D and 2oo3, designed toward SIL 3 and SIL 4 targets

firmware applications 🗺️ M · designed toward the targets, no certification claimed

The vote sits on the shutdown path (LSM STO and the brake), not in the 10 kHz force loop. STO acts on the LSM only; a de-energised zero-power suspension diverges in an indeterminate direction, so landing is an energised safety function with its own monitored reserve and a mechanical backstop, voted separately. There is no FMEDA, no hardware and no WCET measurement.

1oo2D: two diverse lockstep channels
2oo3: a third channel and a hard-wired voter

Diversity comes from different silicon vendors and instruction sets, different schedulers, different code (fabrail-exo-safety is not fabrail-exo) and different arithmetic (integers against f32). Compiler diversity is weak: Ferrocene and HighTec are both LLVM-based. TMS570 (big-endian BE32) cannot be targeted by Rust (EXOTIC §S3, §S4).

Mid-value select

mvs(a, b, c) = max(min(a, b), min(max(a, b), c)). The output always lies between two of the inputs, so one faulty channel, whatever its value, cannot move it outside the range of the two healthy ones.
