Seismic early warning · land-first

The earthquake arrives twice.

An earthquake sends a fast, weak P-wave (a pressure wave in the rock) ahead of the slower S-wave that carries most of the shaking. The gap between them grows by 0.119 s for every kilometre of hypocentral distance. The rail listens for the first arrival and uses the gap to stop and land its carriages. Close to the source there is no gap to use.

Scope, stated honestly.

  • No useful on-site warning closer than about 20 km. The on-site lead is 0.119 s per km of hypocentral distance minus the 1.2 s decision time: about 0 s at 10.1 km and 1.18 s at 20 km. That is enough to freeze (within 100 ms of the pick) but not to land before the S-wave.
  • Every network alert on this page is synthetic or injected. fabrail consumes alerts; it is not an early-warning provider, and a live ShakeAlert or JMA feed is roadmap.
  • The seismic action is not a rated safety function (SAFETY.md, SF-08). It changes the state the rail is in when the shaking arrives. It does not reduce the shaking or shield the building.
  • All records are synthetic (Boore stochastic method, with the P phase scaled so Pd follows Wu–Zhao). The Pd and τc thresholds are calibration values that have not been validated on real records.

Two wavefronts, one house

The fault ruptures at depth. Both waves leave at once; the P-wave travels at 6 km/s, the S-wave at 3.5 km/s. A structure-mounted accelerometer in the house picks the P arrival, and 1.2 s later the detector has enough signal to decide. A regional network may also send an alert about 8 s after the origin. Whichever decision comes first starts the class actions; what is left before the S-wave is the lead. Move the house with the sliders below.

Lead time against distance

Two sources of warning compete. On site, the lead is the S−P time minus the decision time. From a network, it is the S travel time minus the alert latency, independent of Vp. They are equal at Vp·(tnetwork − ton-site) = 40.8 km: closer than that the local pick wins, farther the network does. Below about 20 km neither leaves enough time to land.

Picking the P-wave

The accelerometer sits on the structure or the rail, never on the carriage, whose own motion would look like an earthquake (FAB-SEI-001). Its 500 Hz samples are decimated to 100 Hz and band-passed 0.5–20 Hz. A recursive short-term over long-term average (STA/LTA, 50 over 3000 samples) watches the vertical channel: when the ratio passes 4.0 the P-wave is picked, the carriages freeze within 100 ms (FAB-SEI-009), and the long-term average stops updating so the shaking cannot hide itself. From the pick, double integration gives the ground displacement. Its peak, Pd, grows with magnitude and falls with distance (Wu–Zhao); its dominant period, τc, grows with magnitude (Wu–Kanamori). One second of signal sets the level; three seconds refine it.

Two sources, one decision

The local pick is on site and fast but short-sighted: it needs a second of P-wave to judge the size. A regional network sees the event near the source and can alert before the P-wave reaches a distant house; its alert also carries the origin time and location, from which the S-wave ETA is origin + Rhyp/Vs (within ±1 s in the tests; FAB-SEI-007). Whichever PROTECT comes first starts the class actions.

Local pick, synthetic network alert and the fused state

Time after origin for the event set by the sliders above. The pick delay (0.13 s) is the discrete STA/LTA step response of PHYSICS §20; the network alert is an injected input, labelled synthetic wherever it appears.

  • Alerts can only make the rail safer. A network-only or injected alert may cause FREEZE, LAND, LOCK or a passive ride-through, nothing else. Motion triggered by an alert needs independent confirmation (a local P pick or at least two independent feeds), moves only toward a safer state in the closing direction at unattended limits, and never reverses a user's motion (FAB-EXO-119; SEISMIC §2.3, §5).
  • A cancel is not a restart. It returns to NORMAL only when there is no local evidence of shaking (FAB-SEI-007, FAB-SEI-008).
  • The state machine is one-way until a person looks. NORMAL → TRIGGERED → PROTECT → SHAKING → QUIET (30 s) → INSPECT, and INSPECT needs a manual reset (FAB-SEI-008).

    Why land-first

    A levitated carriage has almost no friction along the rail, which is the point in daily use and the problem in an earthquake. The reasons are physical (PHYSICS §21; SEISMIC §5):

    1. Lateral capacity. Passive reluctance guidance and the guide fin hold roughly 0.1–0.3 g across the rail; design-level floor shaking is 0.3–1 g.
    2. Vertical saturation. An attractive suspension can only pull. When the floor drops faster than gravity can follow, the coil saturates and the carriage lands anyway (FAB-SEI-017).
    3. Travel along the rail. An unforced levitated mass stays put while the floor moves, so it drifts relative to the floor by about the peak ground displacement (FAB-SEI-013). The seat, the locks and the end-zone bumpers hold a landed carriage.
    4. Energy. Landing needs little energy and completes on the supercapacitor even if the supply fails at the S arrival (FAB-SEI-019).
    5. Robust to detector error. A false PROTECT costs a landing. A missed one leaves the mechanics to carry the event, which they must do anyway, because near the source the warning is zero.
    Accelerations that decide the policy

    Ranges in units of g. The guidance capacity is a core design estimate and the floor range is design-level shaking; the LSM authority is Fcont/m at rated mass (WALL 0.020 g, HEAVY 0.010 g), which is why contactless isolation saturates. PHYSICS §6, §21; SEISMIC §4.2.

    The other two readings of "pressure wave"

    The README's "mitigating seismic forces through pressure wave effects" has three readings. Early warning is the one fabrail implements as its default. The other two are research tier and disabled in every preset, and this site says why.

    Mass damping

    A heavy panel driven against the building is a mass damper, but the mass ratio decides: a 5 t panel in a 10⁷ kg building is μ = 0.05 %, negligible next to the 2–5 % damping a building already has. Mass dampers are mainly wind devices.

    Contactless isolation

    A levitated panel could in principle stay still while the floor moves, but the LSM can push a WALL with only 0.02 g against 0.3–1 g of floor shaking, so isolation largely saturates and the bumpers carry the event.

    The villa replay

    A research-tier house choreographer closes open drawers toward their seats before the S-wave, only after a local pick confirms the alert, with every residual reported.
