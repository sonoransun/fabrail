Flagship example · villa_quake

A house that closes its own drawers.

A hillside villa in a synthetic M7.2 at 60 km. After the alert, the house choreographer closes open drawers toward their seats under unattended limits, lands the pantry door, lands and locks the salon wall, and reports every device it could not secure, with the reason.

🧪 R Research tier overall, with a badge on every panel. Simulated replay. Every alert on this page is synthetic/injected, not a live EEW feed. The house choreographer is not a rated safety function.

What the replay shows

Each panel carries the badge of the weakest requirement it demonstrates (gallery rule). Numbers come from the run; the preview fills only the panels that closed forms can fill honestly.

Timeline against tP and tS

Coloured bars: a device moving toward its target SafetyRank, ending when it is secured. Grey: an action whose timing only the run can give. Hatched red: not secured before the deadline (the device takes the core class action and is reported NotSecured with its reason). The deadline is the 5 % quantile of the predicted S arrival minus 1 s. Calculated means a deterministic plan computed at alert time, every deadline met or the miss reported, byte-identical on replay.

Drawer position

Open drawers close toward the seat at the unattended limit v ≤ min(vcap, 150 N/√(mgov·kseries)), 0.230 m/s at 48 kg. Smooth means a C² reference, peak jerk ≤ jmax, arrival ≤ 1 mm/s and overshoot ≤ 0.02 mm in quiet conditions (floor below 0.01 g).

Distance to go, semilog

Log-damped: during capture the distance to go is db·e−t/τ, so ln d is a straight line of slope −1/τ (the requirement asks R² ≥ 0.999). The fit appears with the run.

Contents on the vector friction cone

–

A sliding block slips when abs(ah) > μs·(g + az), with ah the drawer's x acceleration and the floor's y acceleration rotated by the device heading. Stemware gets a rocking flag when abs(ah) > g·b/h. Toppling is not modelled.

μ values are [UNCERTAIN] and are swept ±30 % in the run.

Peak cone utilisation per item in the run (1 = slip onset)

The only wording used for a zero count is "no Coulomb slip event in the sliding-block model"; it says nothing about chips, cracks or toppling.

Compensation-stack waterfall

Peak horizontal and vertical acceleration at each stage of the stack: ground → floor (building configuration) → drawer body (an x-only stage: the drawer acts along its rail, y is the floor's) → contents. Values come from the run; there are no illustrative bars.

Paired runs, inertial smoothing on and off (seed 42)

The fixed-base building must show an x-slip reduction with smoothing on. On the isolated building the no-slip result is credited to the isolation, not to the smoothing; y-slip and rocking are reported as they come.

House DC bus power

Choreographed moves are staggered, deadline-constrained and parallel, so the summed bus power (summed in device index order at every sync step) stays within the budget stated in the scene.

    How the house decides

    • Only toward a higher rank. An alert may command motion only toward a higher SafetyRank (Seated or Landed+Locked > Closed-levitated > Standoff/Hold > Frozen-open) and only in the closing or seating direction: no alert-initiated opening, unlocking, de-isolating or speeding up, and never a reversal of a user's own motion (FAB-EXA-006).
    • Independent confirmation. Alert-triggered motion needs a local P pick or at least two independent feeds; one network feed alone can only freeze, land or lock.
    • Unattended limits. Obstruction detection stays armed, one attempt only: a hand in a closing drawer is detected within 20 ms at no more than 150 N, the drawer does not re-close, it is marked NotSecured and the plan continues (FAB-EXA-011).
    • Egress stays open. The pantry door is on the egress path: it stops, lands and releases ASC, and never locks. The salon wall is off the egress path and lands and locks.
    • Seated drawers stay seated. A seated drawer never unseats on an alert. Inertial smoothing (following only the low-frequency floor motion) is research tier and only for drawers already open when the alert arrives (FAB-HAP-017 to 019).
    • Near field. At 15 km the S−P time is about 2.3 s: a drawer already gliding closed continues to its seat under unattended limits; one gliding open freezes within 100 ms and holds, never reversed (FAB-EXA-014).
    • Restore. Nothing re-opens until QUIET, a manual INSPECT reset and a local restore command; remote-only commands are rejected, and devices re-open one at a time on log-damped open termini (FAB-EXA-015).

    Scope, stated honestly.

    • This is a simulated replay: a synthetic ground motion through a building model, one-way coupled to the devices. Nothing here is a measurement.
    • The alert is synthetic/injected, not a live EEW feed; fabrail is not an early-warning provider, and near the source the warning is about zero.
    • The house choreographer is research tier and not a rated safety function; the land-first core behaviour is the default for every class.
    • Contents results come from a sliding-block model on a friction cone. A zero count is worded "no Coulomb slip event in the sliding-block model"; toppling, chipping and breakage are not modelled.
    • Hero devices run on the Carriage6 plant, the tier on which the simulator and the firmware image are claimed to run the same control code bit for bit (voltage coil mode); the other devices run on the Travel1d plant.
    • Floor prediction in the scene is an oracle (the building's own response); a deployed system would predict it from the alert through a ground-motion model.
    • Conventional isolation is a linear-equivalent model (🔶); HAVEN-F is a research-tier reduced platform (🧪). No building is levitated.
