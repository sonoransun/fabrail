Motion

Stopping is a curve.

Every move is planned from the kinetic energy and momentum a person could meet, and every arrival is log-damped.

A move to a travel end has three parts. A jerk-limited S-curve launches the carriage to a cruise speed that the governor sets from the rated mass. At a precomputed distance the terminus takes over: a constant-jerk blend, then an exponential capture in which the distance to go shrinks by the same fraction every τ seconds, then a short quintic that lands with zero speed and zero acceleration. On a logarithmic axis the capture is a straight line, and that is what "log-damped" means here.

Distance to go, on a log scale

During the capture the distance to go is db·e−t/τ: a straight line on this axis. The dashed line is the closed-form terminus from the measured handover speed (PHYSICS §16.1). FAB-HAP-013 asks a semilog fit with R² ≥ 0.999 on Carriage6 while the floor is quiet.

Speed during the move

The cruise sits under two lines: the class cap from 1.69 J and 22.3 kg·m/s at the governor mass, and the governor's planning cap at 0.90 of that energy (≈ 0.949 of the speed) and 0.95 of that momentum; the supervisor plans 2 % inside it (PHYSICS §16).

The S-curve and the stopping distance

Acceleration never jumps: jerk is bounded, so the acceleration ramps up, holds, and ramps down, and so does the deceleration. Seven segments in all. The same limits fix how far the carriage needs to stop from speed v: dstop = v²/(2a) + v·a/(2j) when v ≥ a²/j, else v·√(v/j).

The governor: rated mass, not hopeful mass

The speed caps come from a kinetic-energy limit of 1.69 J and a momentum limit of 22.3 kg·m/s, the low-energy basis of power-operated pedestrian doors (checked in simulation against numeric limits from ANSI/BHMA A156.19-2019 and A156.38-2019). The governor evaluates them with mgov = max(mrated, 1.05·m̂): the estimate m̂ can only make the carriage slower, never faster, so a mass estimate sabotaged to half the true mass changes nothing (FAB-SAF-001). The governor plans at 0.90 of the energy cap and 0.95 of the momentum cap, which leaves a margin for tracking error and rail disturbances; the independent integer monitor trips on a cap only while the drive itself does positive work, and a drawer thrown past its cap by hand is met by governor damping, not by a trip.

Pinch force: F = v·√(m·k)

If something is caught at the closing edge, the carriage's kinetic energy goes into squeezing it. For a body part of stiffness k the peak quasi-static force is F = √(2E·k) = v·√(m·k). That is why the energy cap matters more than any speed number, and why an edge seal (a soft spring in series with the hand) cuts the force. The directional clamp separately bounds the stall force the drive may apply.

What this page shows, and what it does not

  • Live, in WebAssembly: the moves on the stage are the real supervisor, S-curve planner, governor, terminus and cascade on the Carriage6 rigid-body plant (six degrees of freedom, co-energy magnets, LSM, contacts, sensor noise) in voltage coil mode. The dashed reference lines are closed forms.
  • Without the engine the stage animates the closed-form reference of the same move (formulas.js closeProfile), labelled as such: that is the path the controller is asked to follow, not a simulation of the carriage.
  • WALL and HEAVY moves are slow by design (0.0446 and 0.025 m/s); they appear here only in the closed-form sections.
  • Pinch forces are the quasi-static energy form on linear body springs. They are not measurements, and dB(A) or FEM-validated forces are not claimed anywhere on this site.
