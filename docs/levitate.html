Levitation

Earnshaw says no, so the rail listens.

Permanent magnets carry the weight; coils only correct, and their current falls to zero at equilibrium.

No arrangement of static magnets holds a steel carriage still (Earnshaw, 1842). Pull a magnet a little closer to the rail and its attraction grows faster than the weight, so it snaps up; let it sag and it drops. The hybrid suspension keeps the permanent magnets for the lifting and adds coils that only nudge. Mizuno's zero-power control then walks the gap to the one place where the magnets alone balance the load, so the coil current settles to zero whatever the load is. That is why the gap moves when the load changes.

The gap follows m(g)

At zero coil current a mass m floats where the magnets alone carry it: m(g) = m*·((2g* + h′)/(2g + h′))², a fixed curve per class (PHYSICS §3, FAB-LEV-005). The shaded band is the modified zero-power band: outside it the controller holds the band edge with a bias current instead (FAB-LEV-018). The dot is the live engine.

Gap and coil current, last 12 s

After a load step the gap moves to the new zero-power point and the current returns to zero (FAB-LEV-004 asks abs(i) < 1 mA within 2 s on the DOOR +98.1 N step, 4.5967 mm ± 2 µm). Sensor noise and the ±5 µm gap ripple are part of the simulation.

Why switching it off is never an option

Linearised at the float, the magnets act as a negative spring: the open-loop heave pole is p = √(4g₀/(2g + h′)), a divergence time constant of 1/p. The chart shows what happens after the control is switched off from the live state: the gap departs as cosh(p·t) and the carriage reaches a stop within a fraction of a second. In the real rail this is not a mode anyone can select: the levitation safe state is SS1 Safe Landing, an energised, monitored descent on a supercapacitor, and the propulsion's safe state (STO) never touches the suspension.

Stops: what the magnets can and cannot do on contact

A zero-power hybrid carriage cannot be designed so that it never clamps up: at any gap closer than the float the magnets pull harder than the weight. So both stops are designed contacts. The upper stop is a sliding surface that must carry the magnet pull, at most 1.30 times the weight for DOOR, WALL and HEAVY; the DRAWER uses elastomer upper rollers rated 600 N against a 551.5 N pull. The lower stop is where a landed carriage rests, and it must stay there with power off: the magnets may pull at most 0.90 of the lightest operating weight at the landing gap, so the carriage never lifts itself.

Thermal drift: a warm magnet is a heavier load

NdFeB loses about 0.12 % of its remanence per kelvin. At zero current the float obeys n·Fpm(g) = m·g₀ with Fpm proportional to Br², so a warmer magnet floats closer to the rail, exactly as a heavier load would, and the coil current still returns to zero: Δg = (2g* + h′)·αBr·ΔT/2.

Float-gap shift against magnet temperature

αBr = −0.0012 /K (PHYSICS §12). Anchors for +40 K: −0.195 / −0.416 / −0.891 mm (DRAWER / DOOR / WALL and HEAVY), FAB-LEV-014; the dynamic shift is checked on Heave1 with a compressed ramp and abs(i) < 5 mA.

What this page shows, and what it does not

  • Live, in WebAssembly: the zero-power float, the load step, m̂ convergence, the band clamp and the PayloadMismatch degrade run the Rust controller (fabrail-core through fabrail-rt, the firmware's group code) on the Heave1 plant tier: one vertical degree of freedom, co-energy magnets, voltage-mode coils with eddy lag, sensor noise. Heave1 has no travel axis; the full rigid body (Carriage6) is on the Glide and Touch pages.
  • The payload is emulated. The browser ABI has no mass input, so a payload Δm is applied as a steady vertical floor acceleration az = g₀·Δm/m*. The downward force on the body and the zero-power equilibrium are identical; the inertia stays m*, and the structure accelerometer also sees az, which is why the page ramps it slowly.
  • Control off is a closed-form model in JavaScript (coil current 0, no eddy lag, no dissipation, rigid stops). The engine itself is never switched off.
  • HEAVY runs on Heave1 here, the tier its acceptance uses (with Travel1d for travel); the HEAVY six-degree-of-freedom model is not used on this page.
  • Nothing here is a hardware measurement, and no standard is claimed. "Zero friction" is a direction: the modelled equivalent friction of a levitated carriage is about 1e-3 to 1e-2, never 0.
